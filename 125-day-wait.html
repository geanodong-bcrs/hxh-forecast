What the wait was spent on

The ch. 411-420 line is flat across the whole window: there was nothing left to report, because every one of its ten chapters was already finished. The work in those 125 days went to ch. 421-430, which climbed from 4.30 to 6.20 chapter-equivalents on thirteen separate reports.

One of those matters more than the rest. Chapter 421 — the first chapter of the next batch — reached manuscript complete on 26 May 2026, thirty-four days before ch. 411 went on sale. So at the moment Shueisha started serializing ch. 411-420, it was holding a finished batch plus six chapters of the batch after it, with the next batch’s opener already in hand.

Is 125 days normal? It has happened twice

The obvious way to ask is “when did the batch become finished, and how long then until it went on sale?” That question has no stable answer, because “finished” needs a line drawn and the answer moves with it.

Measure instead the thing that needs no threshold — the silence between a run’s last public report and its on-sale date:

Two of the three runs show the same shape: reporting stops near the end, the run goes quiet for months, the next batch advances, and only then does the magazine publish. Ch. 401-410 is the clearer case of the two — 54 reports on ch. 411–420 during its silence, against 10 on ch. 421–430 during the other.

Ch. 391-400 is the exception, and an instructive one: six days from its last report to the shelves, with no work reported on the next batch at all. It was also the return from a 1428-day hiatus, so it is arguably not comparable to anything.

Why the threshold matters

An earlier version of this page said ch. 411-420 was the only run ever held back. That was true only at a cut of exactly B=10.00, and it was the wrong way to look. Ch. 401-410 went quiet at 9.50 and reached 10.00 on 2024-11-16, five weeks after it started serialising. Where you put the line decides the answer:

What does hold still: the on-sale block

Shueisha’s side of this barely moves. Ten chapters, nine or ten weeks, six runs in a row. Everything that varies is the gap between them.

That is the firmest thing in the record: a fixed serialisation block with a variable gap in front of it. It is consistent with a magazine holding its rhythm steady and letting the hiatus absorb whatever pace the author sets — but consistent-with is not evidence-for, and the next section says why this cannot currently be pushed further.

Why the obvious next question cannot be answered yet. If the magazine is waiting on anything, the natural candidate is the next batch reaching some level of readiness. When ch. 401-410 went on sale the next run stood at exactly 5.00 of 10, crossed eight days earlier — a striking fit. For ch. 411-420 it stood at 6.20, having passed 5.00 fully 108 days before. No single trigger level fits both. And the record cannot be extended backwards to settle it: Togashi’s first production post is from May 2022, so ch. 361-390 have no readiness data of any kind, and ch. 391-400 was the return from a four-year hiatus. That leaves two usable transitions. Two points do not identify a rule, and this page is not going to pretend otherwise — it is a hypothesis for ch. 421-430 to test, not a finding.
